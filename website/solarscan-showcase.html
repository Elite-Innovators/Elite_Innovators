HOW IT WORKS

Address in. Feasibility report out.

Five stages, fully automated, running on the same imagery a vendor would eventually collect by hand.

01

Address → Imagery

The address is geocoded and matched to the highest-resolution satellite / aerial tile available for that footprint, cropped to the parcel boundary.

geocoding + tile fetch
02

Roof segmentation

A segmentation model traces the usable roof polygon — separating flat roof from overhangs, water tanks, stairwell heads, and shared walls — then computes true usable area in m².

U-Net / SAM
03

Shadow & sun-path modelling

Neighbouring building heights are estimated and projected against the sun's path across the year, producing an hour-by-hour shading map over the roof polygon.

sun-path geometry
04

Energy & financial modelling

Usable area and shading combine with irradiance modelling to estimate kWh/year, optimal panel count, subsidy-adjusted system cost under PM Surya Ghar, and payback period in years.

pvlib irradiance model
05

Vendor-ready report

Every number is compiled into a single-page PDF — roof diagram, shading summary, system size, cost breakdown — formatted so a solar vendor can quote directly from it.

one-page PDF
HARD CORE

Shade isn't guesswork — it's geometry.

Most self-serve solar calculators assume a flat, unobstructed roof. Real roofs sit next to water tanks, taller neighbours, and stairwell heads that cast moving shadows through the day and across seasons.

Neighbour-height estimation

Adjacent structures are measured from imagery to build a 3D obstruction model around the roof polygon.

Annual sun-path projection

Shadow length and direction are simulated across solstices and equinoxes, not just a single noon snapshot.

Irradiance-adjusted yield

Shaded hours are subtracted from modelled irradiance before the kWh/year figure is ever shown to a homeowner.
